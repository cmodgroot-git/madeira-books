# Histórias da Madeira — people in a moving vehicle

Use this for any page that shows people travelling in a moving vehicle (car, bus, taxi, cable car, boat cabin), from inside or outside. It adds to `madeira-book-illustrations` (picture), `madeira-book-highlights` (tap regions) and `madeira-scene-life` (animation); follow those as usual and use this for the moving-interior parts. Applied to book 6 pages 2 (tunnel) and 3 (fog, wipers); book 7 page 8, book 4 page 1 and book 11 page 8 use the outside view. For boats, paragliders and other outdoor craft, see "Make it physically right" in `madeira-book-illustrations`. The motion code (`vehbump.py`) is at the end of this skill.

## 1. The picture
**Choose the camera by what the page needs.**
- **Inside, from the middle of the back seat** when the page is about the drive (the road, tunnel, fog, wipers, lights, the dog on the back seat). The women are seen from behind; faces don't show.
- **Outside, from the road ahead, looking at the car through its windscreen** when a face must show (a smile, a look at a phone, talking, someone asleep on a shoulder in the back). Everyone then faces us naturally, belts over their outer shoulders, and the seating problems below cannot happen. No side-window motion on these pages; animate the sea, sky or sun instead (`madeira-scene-life`; book 11 page 8: the sea through the side window ripples on a drawn polygon kept clear of the window pillar). A van seen this way shows the front seats and the back bench behind them (book 11 page 8: Rita in front with her cup and medal, Tiago driving, Ana asleep on João's shoulder behind).
- Don't try an over-the-shoulder view from the back seat to show a front passenger's face: Krea turned her backwards every time (book 7 page 8, five tries).

**Inside: describe what the camera sees, not where people sit.** Krea ignores "she sits in her seat" and seats people beside or in front of the seats, facing the camera. What worked (book 6 pages 2 and 3, seed 8104), written into the scene:
"What the camera sees: the view from the middle of the back seat of a small car, looking forward over the two front seats toward the windscreen. The two front seat backs, upholstered in smooth plain warm grey fabric, are the biggest shapes in the picture: they rise from the bottom edge and fill the lower half, mirror images of each other, with a gap between them showing the centre console and gear lever. On top of each seat back sits an identical tall padded grey headrest on two metal posts, raised to head height. Each woman sits upright in the middle of her own seat, her body exactly behind her own seat back, and the back of her head rests against the middle of her own headrest: the headrest covers the lower half of the back of her head and her neck; above it we see the upper half of her head from behind; her hair falls down beside the headrest. Their faces are turned away from us, toward the windscreen."
Negative prompt additions: "faces toward the viewer, person facing backward, visible chest, visible lap, person beside the seat, person leaning into the middle, head floating above the headrest, low headrest, seatbelt across the back, seatbelt on the window, sideways passenger, driver looking at the passenger, steering wheel on the right, speckled upholstery".
Give only the style and character-sheet references; a car picture as a third reference made the upholstery mottled and the fog disappear. Keep one car across a book by reusing the same scene wording and seed instead.

**Cable cars (book 5 Funchal–Monte, book 9 Garajau).** Two views worked; floating outside views did not.
- **Inside the cabin** (book 5 page 7): the women on a bench by a big window, the view outside showing where they are.
- **At the station** (book 9 pages 1 and 8): the friends stepping in or out at the top station platform, railings on both sides, the platform ending at the door sill on a short piece of volcanic rock, the car hanging just past the edge, its single cable running out through the air toward the far station; the landmark (Cristo Rei, facing the sea) in the near distance. Show boarding by drawing the person from behind with one foot inside.
- Avoid a gondola seen from outside in mid-air: after many tries Krea kept adding extra cars and cables, cables through the windows, the car pushing the cable up, wrong angles and people in a row on one bench. A small four-seat cabin has two benches facing each other; describe it as "a smaller, simpler version of" the book 5 cabin and give that page as the third reference.

**Left-hand drive, both views.** Portugal drives on the right; the steering wheel is on the car's left. From inside (looking forward) the driver is on the left of the picture; from outside (car facing us) the driver is on the right of the picture. Krea often draws right-hand drive: say it in the prompt ("This is a left-hand-drive European car… the driver's seat is on the right side of the picture") and check it. A whole-picture flip fixes a wrong driver side only when the scenery is also wrong the same way; otherwise ask Krea to swap the people.

**Parked vehicles seen from outside** (book 10 page 1, a van at the kerb; book 14 page 1, Rita's pale-blue hatchback on a hilltop): left-hand drive shows as the driver's door on the street side. Draw the vehicle in perspective with the facade (roof below the first-floor balconies when it's a small van) and the driver standing by the open driver's door or the front corner.

**Real geography.** Put the sea, cliffs and sun where they really are for the trip: on the north coast the sea is to the north, so going west to Porto Moniz the sea is on the car's right, and coming back east toward Funchal it's on the car's left with the sunset behind the car. Coastal roads are asphalt with white edge lines, not cobbles (add "cobbles, cobblestones, paving stones" to the negative prompt).

**Before the user sees an interior picture, check:**
1. each head is behind its own headrest, which hides the lower back of the head (no head floating above or beside it, nobody facing backward);
2. each belt comes out of a solid door pillar, the same height on both sides, and goes over the outer shoulder (driver's left, passenger's right) — never anchored on glass, never across a back;
3. the side windows show the same place as the windscreen (lit tunnel walls inside a tunnel, fog and railing on a foggy viaduct);
4. the driver looks at the road;
5. both front seats and headrests are identical and mirrored; oncoming traffic is in the left lane.
If one fails, regenerate with better scene wording; don't patch seats or belts with edits or mirrored composites (that produced a belt anchored on the window, book 6).

**Other rules:** the dog is in the back seat (on a blanket, or sitting looking forward), never in front. The windscreen shows the road ahead with the painted wipers at rest along the bottom. Dials and signs follow the illustration skill's rule (blank in the prompt, painted in code).

## 2. Masks (master px 1600×898)
- **Windscreen glass:** the tap region for the glass word (e.g. `chuva`) or a hand polygon inside the frame, minus people (SAM, dilated 5–9 px), the rear-view mirror and the wiper strip.
- **Side panes:** draw each pane as a hand polygon from a 2× gridded crop, then **keep only real glass by colour** so frames, divider bars, pillars and belts never move. Sample colours first:
  - plants and cliffs: HSV hue 16–60, sat > 35, value > 80;
  - road and its white line: value > 105, sat < 48;
  - sea and pink-purple sky: blue ≥ green − 2 and luminance > 85;
  - tunnel walls and fog: value > 110 — but warm-lit tunnel walls share the belts' tan hue (book 6 page 2), so there trace the panes and the belts by hand (belt = a 40 px line from anchor to shoulder) and only drop dark frame pixels (luminance < 90);
  - exclude tan (hue ≤ 18, sat > 38: belts, trim) and dark (value < 70: frames).
  Then open 3–5 px, close 5–9 px, erode 3–5 px, intersect with the polygon, and drop components under 300–400 px. Look at it in red: SAM alone grabbed pillars, roof lining and hair.
- Re-cut every mask and the tap regions whenever the picture changes (file names and cache: Project facts).

## 3. The motion (approved settings)
Everything outside the car moves; the car, people, seats, belts and dog never do.
1. **Road bump:** the whole outside view (windscreen and side glass together) shifts as if the car rode an uneven road. Damped bumps `a·e^(−6u)·sin(2π·7u + π/2)` at ¼·T×(0.55, 2.35, 3.3) with amplitudes 2.6, 1.6 and 0.9 px, plus 0.35 px of gentle sway and a rotation of about 0.06°. No blur. Remap with a **plate** in which a 21 px band outside the glass is inpainted from the glass, so shifting never pulls frame pixels in.
2. **Side windows:** only here do we show speed. Each glass row becomes a seamless band of its own pixels (period P=150, K=50 crossfade; never mirrored, since mirroring zig-zags railings and road edges) scrolling toward the back of the car (left windows: −1, right: +1), 2 cycles per loop.
   - **Graded blur:** sharp at the window's front edge (toward the windscreen), increasing toward the back edge: σ = 1 + 15·t^1.3, where t runs 0→1 from front to back. Blend precomputed masked horizontal blurs at σ 0, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16 (blur `layer*mask` and `mask`, then divide).
   - **Opacity:** blend over the still painting at **70%** (`OPA=.7`); the user found 100% too strong and 50% too weak.
3. **Continuous lines along the direction of travel stay crisp** (the white road-edge line). Fit a straight line to the bright low-saturation pixels, draw it as an anti-aliased band at 4× scale (half-width = 90th percentile of the residuals), extend it across neighbouring panes, and keep it static on top of the moving view. Left in, it breaks into dashes and staircases.
4. **Things on the car move with the car, after the bump:** wipers and their freshly wiped arc. Erase the painted wipers by copying glass from about 32 px *above* (`np.roll(B, +32, 0)`, then blur the seam). Draw the blades from pivots and lengths read off the painted ones. They sweep up over the first 32% of the loop and down by 64%, then rest; cosine easing, about 100°, 2.4 s per cycle. Each sweep leaves a clearer arc (`img + .35·(img − blur12)`, ×0.97) fading with `exp(−dt/1.1)·.85`. Without animated wipers, keep the painted wipers still by cutting the windscreen polygon above them.
5. **Things outside move with the bump, before it:** tunnel lights pulse in a wave toward the car (phase by distance from the vanishing point, 3 cycles per 1.6 s).
6. **One layer per page:** combine the bump, the side windows and any wipers or lights into a single loop whose length fits every cycle: T = 4.0 s with N = 40, or T = 4.8 s with N = 48 when wipers (2.4 s) or lights (1.6 s) share it. The sheet is about 0.9–1.4 MB.

## Code (`vehbump.py`)
Save this as `vehbump.py` if it isn't in the scratchpad. A page script builds the masks (section 2), then calls `build`, saves one layer and inserts it:
```python
frames = vehbump.build(B, GLASS, [(SIDE_L, -1, front_x, back_x), (SIDE_R, +1, front_x, back_x)],
                       lines=vehbump.crisp_line(B, SIDE_L, band_y, maxx), outside=lights_fn, car=wipers_fn, N=48, T=4.8, OPA=.7)
```

`outside(img, t)` changes the view before the bump (tunnel lights); `car(img, t)` draws things fixed to the car after it (wipers). Leave out what a page doesn't need.
```python
import numpy as np, cv2
SIG=[0,2,4,7,11,16]
def bump(t,t0,a,f=7.,dmp=6.):
    u=t-t0
    return 0. if u<0 else a*np.exp(-dmp*u)*np.sin(2*np.pi*f*u+np.pi/2)
def jolt(t,T,hits=((0.55,2.6),(2.35,1.6),(3.3,0.9))):
    s=T/4.0
    dy=sum(bump(t+o,t0*s,a) for o in (0,T) for t0,a in hits)+.35*np.sin(2*np.pi*t*3/T)
    rot=np.deg2rad(.06*np.sin(2*np.pi*t/T)+.05*sum(bump(t+o,hits[0][0]*s,1.) for o in (0,T)))
    return dy,rot
def plate(B,glass):
    band=cv2.dilate(glass,np.ones((21,21),np.uint8))&(1-glass)
    return cv2.inpaint(np.clip(B,0,255).astype(np.uint8),band,9,cv2.INPAINT_TELEA).astype(np.float32)
def side_frames(PL,mask,dirn,front,back,N,cycles=2,P=150,K=50):
    H,W=mask.shape; GX=np.arange(W)[None,:].repeat(H,0).astype(np.float32)
    rows=[]
    for y in range(H):
        xs=np.nonzero(mask[y])[0]
        if len(xs)<6: continue
        for rr in np.split(xs,np.nonzero(np.diff(xs)>1)[0]+1):
            if len(rr)<6: continue
            xa,xb=rr[0],rr[-1]+1
            seg=cv2.resize(PL[y:y+1,xa:xb],(P+K,1),interpolation=cv2.INTER_LINEAR)[0]
            w=np.linspace(0,1,K)[:,None]; tile=seg[:P].copy(); tile[:K]=seg[P:P+K]*(1-w)+seg[:K]*w
            rows.append((y,xa,xb,tile))
    ys,xs=np.nonzero(mask); y0,y1,x0,x1=max(0,ys.min()-4),min(H,ys.max()+5),max(0,xs.min()-30),min(W,xs.max()+31)
    mm=mask[y0:y1,x0:x1].astype(np.float32); gx=GX[y0:y1,x0:x1]
    tpos=np.clip((front-gx)/(front-back),0,1) if front>back else np.clip((gx-front)/(back-front),0,1)
    idx=np.interp(1+15*tpos**1.3,SIG,np.arange(len(SIG))); lo=np.floor(idx).astype(int); hi=np.minimum(lo+1,len(SIG)-1); fr=(idx-lo)[...,None]
    out=[]
    for k in range(N):
        lay=PL[y0:y1,x0:x1].copy()
        for y,xa,xb,tile in rows:
            xx=np.arange(xa,xb); off=k*cycles*P/N*dirn
            lay[y-y0,xa-x0:xb-x0]=tile[np.mod(np.round((xx-xa)*P/(xb-xa)-off).astype(int),P)]
        st=[lay]
        for s in SIG[1:]:
            num=cv2.GaussianBlur(lay*mm[...,None],(0,0),sigmaX=s,sigmaY=.6); den=cv2.GaussianBlur(mm,(0,0),sigmaX=s,sigmaY=.6)[...,None]
            st.append(num/np.maximum(den,1e-3))
        st=np.stack(st,0)
        res=np.take_along_axis(st,lo[None,...,None].repeat(3,-1),0)[0]*(1-fr)+np.take_along_axis(st,hi[None,...,None].repeat(3,-1),0)[0]*fr
        out.append(((y0,y1,x0,x1),res,mm))
    return out
def build(B,glass,sides,lines=None,outside=None,car=None,N=40,T=4.0,OPA=.7,cycles=2):
    H,W=glass.shape; GX,GY=np.meshgrid(np.arange(W,dtype=np.float32),np.arange(H,dtype=np.float32))
    PL=plate(B,glass)
    SF=[(m,side_frames(PL,m,d,f,b,N,cycles)) for m,d,f,b in sides]
    frames=[]
    for k in range(N):
        t=k/N*T; img=PL.copy()
        for m,fs in SF:
            (y0,y1,x0,x1),res,mm=fs[k]; sub=img[y0:y1,x0:x1]
            sub[:]=np.where(mm[...,None]>0,PL[y0:y1,x0:x1]*(1-OPA)+res*OPA,sub)
        if lines is not None: img=img*(1-lines[...,None])+PL*lines[...,None]
        if outside is not None: img=outside(img,t)
        dy,rot=jolt(t,T); cx,cy=W/2,H/3; c,s=np.cos(rot),np.sin(rot)
        mx=cx+(GX-cx)*c-(GY-cy)*s; my=cy+(GX-cx)*s+(GY-cy)*c-dy
        img=cv2.remap(img,mx.astype(np.float32),my.astype(np.float32),cv2.INTER_LINEAR,borderMode=cv2.BORDER_REFLECT)
        if car is not None: img=car(img,t)
        frames.append(img)
    return frames
def crisp_line(B,pane,band_y=330,maxx=None):
    """fit a straight anti-aliased band to a bright, low-saturation road line inside a pane"""
    H,W=pane.shape; hsv=cv2.cvtColor(np.clip(B,0,255).astype(np.uint8),cv2.COLOR_RGB2HSV)
    GY=np.arange(H)[:,None].repeat(W,1)
    L=((hsv[...,2]>150)&(hsv[...,1]<45)&(pane>0)&(GY>band_y)).astype(np.uint8)
    n,lb,st,_=cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats(L); L=np.isin(lb,[i for i in range(1,n) if st[i,2]>25])
    ys,xs=np.nonzero(L)
    if len(xs)<30: return np.zeros((H,W),np.float32)
    k,c=np.linalg.lstsq(np.vstack([xs,np.ones_like(xs)]).T,ys,rcond=None)[0]; half=max(3.,np.percentile(np.abs(ys-(k*xs+c)),90))
    big=np.zeros((H*4,W*4),np.uint8); x0,x1=xs.min()-30,(maxx if maxx else xs.max()+30)
    cv2.line(big,(int(x0*4),int((k*x0+c)*4)),(int(x1*4),int((k*x1+c)*4)),255,int(half*8),cv2.LINE_AA)
    return cv2.GaussianBlur(cv2.resize(big,(W,H),interpolation=cv2.INTER_AREA).astype(np.float32)/255*pane,(0,0),.7)
```
