## The reader (the user's standing rules)
- Adults who don't speak Portuguese yet, learning **European Portuguese** by comprehension: the picture shows the meaning, never a translation. Meanings exist only as a hidden fallback.
- **Continuation matters most.** Each book builds on every earlier one: it reuses known words in new combinations and adds a measured set of new ones. A reader who finished book N must be able to read book N+1 with only its new words unfamiliar.
- **Grow toward adult sophistication.** Day-to-day life in Madeira with Rita (and Ana and the dog), but the language and the stories should mature book by book — never childish, never a vocabulary list in disguise.
- **Enjoyable and engaging.** The user's goal: stories readers enjoy and want to continue. Give each book a small hook (a tease, a surprise, a question left open) and carry threads across books.
- **Modern Madeira, the 2010s** (the user's choice): the stories are set in present-day Madeira so they can bring in modern words and actions. Characters use smartphones (alarms, messages, photos, maps, calling, paying by card or phone), drive 2010s cars with touchscreens, take the modern buses, shop in supermarkets and glass-fronted shops, book online, and move through the modern city (the marina, the waterfront avenue, apartment blocks and hotels, the airport) as well as what still stands today (old-town cobbles, the market, the cable car, basket sledges, levadas). Use the vocabulary that goes with it where the story invites it (*telemóvel, mensagem, aplicação, cartão, multibanco, ecrã, carregar, reservar, Wi-Fi*). Nothing in the text or pictures should feel decades out of date.
- **Real places look real.** When the user asks for a setting closer to the real thing, the pictures work from their photos of the place (`madeira-book-illustrations`, "Real places"). If the text names something the real view doesn't show (the sea from the Faial kart track), ask the user how to show it rather than changing the text silently.
- **The reading experience** (built into the page; keep it working, see `madeira-book-highlights`):
  - Tap a word: its thing lights up and its own clean recording plays (from `words.mp3`, never cut from a sentence). Long-press (phone) or Ctrl/⌘/⌥-click shows the meaning.
  - Action words have a wavy underline and play an action cue (move, hold, rest, spin, sound, arrow) when tapped.
  - Reader buttons, in this order: **Slow** (on at every visit, the user's choice; plays narration at 0.75), **Listen** (label changes to **Stop** while playing and resets when the page changes; L / space bar), **Whole book** (off by default, remembered; after a page ends it turns the page ~1 s later, starts reading ~0.9 s after that, and at the last page opens Words & quiz; any tap, Stop or manual page turn cancels it).
  - Listen highlights each word ~0.35 s before it is spoken. Action words during Listen (the user's choice): with Slow on, every action word is read at a further 0.65× and plays its full cue; with Slow off, an action word that is new on this page plays a short cue (about 0.8 s, class `actq` on the picture) at normal speed, and an action met before only lights up. Tapping always plays the full cue.
  - Opening a book always starts on page 1. Page 1 shows the book title (title font) just above the text, and the header title is hidden there. Opening a book is silent (the user's choice); the title is read only as part of Listen: on page 1, Listen (and Whole book) reads the title with each title word lit as it is spoken, then the page.
  - **Words & quiz** per book: every new word as a picture close-up (cropped from its page), and an 8-question picture quiz that never shows two look-alike close-ups in one quiz; missed words come back in later quizzes.
  - Library header: language picker and a **How to use** button; the help ends with **Start with book 1**.
  - On page entry the picture and the text are both on screen (a side-by-side spread on wide screens, stacked on narrow ones), on the page's own background colour.

## Step 1 — take stock of what the reader knows
Read the artifact (`pages1`, `pages2`, …) and build the known-word list: every `[shown|key]` key, plus the untagged function words and verb forms already used (é, está, há, tem, vai, com, para, mas, porque, muito…). Note which tenses and structures have appeared. Note open threads (people, places, the dog) to pick up. Count the published files (Artifact `list`, `scope: "files"`) against the 511 limit.

## Step 2 — choose the book's level (the ladder)
Move up one rung per book unless the user asks otherwise; each rung keeps everything from the rungs below.
1. Books 1–4 (done): present tense, simple sentences, *há*, *ir a*, a little direct speech, one fixed past (*Foi um bom dia*).
2. **Book 5 (done):** longer sentences joined with *quando, porque, enquanto, depois de + infinitive*; *ir + infinitive* for plans; more dialogue between characters; a small everyday complication that gets resolved (rain, a closed shop, a missed bus).
3. **Book 6 (done):** pretérito perfeito for a day being told (*ontem fui, comprámos*); opinions and preferences (*acho que, prefiro, gosto mais de*); time expressions.
4. **Book 7 (done):** imperfeito for background and habits (*quando era pequena, chovia*); short reported speech; a bit of Madeiran culture explained in-story (festa, levadas history, wine).
5. **Books 8–12 (done):** conditional and polite requests (*gostaria, podia*), fixed subjunctive phrases (*espero que, talvez, talvez devesse*), *se calhar*, small emotional nuance, humour, a story arc across pages, light narration of thoughts in «…».
6. **Book 13 (done):** the past counterfactual *se soubesse, tinha ficado*; *talvez + subjunctive* (*talvez vejamos*); future subjunctive in a fixed phrase (*se tivermos sorte*).
7. **Book 14 (done):** future subjunctive in time and condition clauses (*quando eu disser, se tiveres medo*), *mesmo que + present subjunctive*; callbacks to earlier books' lines.
8. **Book 15 (done):** imperfect subjunctive in wishes (*quem me dera que as compras fossem…*, *quem me dera que houvesse um elevador*); *embora + present subjunctive* (*embora a casa seja pequena*).
9. **Book 16 (done):** reported speech with tense shift (*vai ensinar-nos → disse que nos ia ensinar*, *perguntou se querias*, *joguei → disse que já tinha jogado*, *trouxe → disse-me que nunca tinha trazido*). Each reported line repeats the original almost word for word, and the original is shown (an earlier page, or a faded inset in the picture) and is tappable through the **echo** feature (Step 5).
10. **Book 17 (done):** *se + imperfect subjunctive* for advice and hypotheses (*Se eu fosse a senhora, escolhia…*, *Se eu fosse tu, não estacionava…* — European Portuguese uses the imperfect as the conditional here), *como se fosse*, one mixed clause (*Se eu tivesse lido o sinal, não estava agora aqui*).
11. **Book 18 (text approved, pictures painted and awaiting review, `tools/b18/README.md` has the status):** *caso / para que / antes que* + present subjunctive in a canyoning safety briefing (*Caso fiquem presos…, apitem*; *para que os pés fiquem…*; *antes que mudes de ideias*). Next rungs to choose from: *se + imperfect subjunctive + conditional* in longer hypotheses, passive with *ser*.

Keep each page to 1–3 sentences (about 18–40 words) so it fits beside the picture; length grows with the rung, not past ~45 words.

## Step 3 — draft the text (checkpoint 1: the user approves before any picture)
- 8 pages. About **25–40 new tappable words** per book; every page also reuses several known words, and at least half the tappable words on a page are known ones when possible.
- Only tag words whose thing **can be shown in that page's picture**; actions must be visibly happening in it (see the action rule in `madeira-book-highlights`). Abstract or grammatical words stay untagged.
- Tag a good share of action words (verbs the picture shows happening), since each gets an action cue: movement, hands and body, sleeping, spinning, sound and expression.
- Keys are lowercase ASCII without accents and match the meaning shown (`[curva|curva]` for a single bend, not `curvas`).
- Use European Portuguese forms and vocabulary (*autocarro, pequeno-almoço, casa de banho, telemóvel, sandes*, tu-forms between friends, *a Rita* with the article).
- Keep the setting in the 2010s (see The reader): modern objects and actions where the story naturally has them, never period details.
- Keep continuity: same characters, same props (black leash, straw tote), real Madeira places, realistic weather and season. When a book returns to a place from an earlier book, the pictures must show it from the same viewpoint with the same furniture sizes.
- Note what the characters wear for this book's story (place, weather, activity), so the pictures can choose fitting outfits.
- Show the user: the 8 pages as they'll read, then a table per page of the tagged words (new vs known) and what the picture must show (things and actions). Wait for approval or edits.

## Step 5 — build the book (no stops unless something can't be undone)
1. Add `pagesN` and the `STORIES` entry (new story id, title, cover index), page masters (resized to 1600×898), published JPGs and thumbnails (names in Project facts), `COVER_IMG`, the `PHOTOS` entries (image + dimmer per page), and the new id in `hlmask.py`'s `BOOK` map and `hlspecs.py` (import `conv/hl_book<N>.py`). A small idempotent `build<N>.py` that does all of this (each book since 10 has one; copy the last one and change the ids) makes reruns safe. Check that keys reused from earlier books still carry the right sense in `GLOSS` (`piloto` meant "racing driver", so book 14 used a new key `pilotos` for paraglider pilots; `chao` was widened to "floor, ground"). Add the new story id to Project facts in every Madeira skill.
2. Tap regions with `madeira-book-highlights` (review sheet for every word; flag actions not shown).
3. **Action cues** with `madeira-book-highlights` ("Action cues"): add an entry for every action word that is visibly happening. Use `move` with a clean plate for movement, `arrow` for movement shown by direction only, `hold` for hands and body, `rest` for sleeping or breathing, `spin` (with a clean plate) for spinning round, `sound` for shouts, smiles, claps, waving and expressions. Then run `conv/actfx.py`. The words get the wavy underline automatically.
4. Animation with `madeira-scene-life` (and `madeira-vehicle-interior` inside vehicles): 1–3 simple story-led effects per page where the picture invites them.
5. **Word meanings:** add every new key to `GLOSS` with six meanings in this order — English, German, French, Spanish, Dutch, Polish (`[en,de,fr,es,nl,pl]`) — giving the sense used on that page (e.g. `obrigada` → "thank you (said by a woman)"). Names, places, birds and Madeiran specialities get a short explanation (`espetada` → "espetada (beef skewer)", `penhadaguia` → "Penha d'Águia (Eagle Rock, a huge cliff on Madeira's north coast)", `manta` → "buzzard (Madeiran name)"); parts of fixed phrases name the phrase (`piao` → "spins (dá um pião: spins round)"). Keys already in `GLOSS` from earlier books keep their entry. Check every key in the page text has a `GLOSS` entry.
6. **Page narration (Listen):**
   - Spoken text: the page text with tags removed and quotes and dashes taken out (`audio/narr.py texts` writes all of them to `audio/texts.json`).
   - Record each page with Krea `generate_audio`, model `elevenlabs/tts`, `eleven_v4`, voice Matilda `XrExE9yKIg1WjnnlVkGX`, the text (field `prompt`, not `text`) prefixed with `[European Portuguese accent, from Lisbon, calm storyteller]`. Save as `audio/raw/b<B>p<P>.mp3`. All 8 pages can be recorded in one turn of parallel calls.
   - `audio/narr.py build B:P …` converts each to mono 24 kHz 48 kbit/s `audio/b<B>p<P>.mp3`, force-aligns the known words with `audio/align.py` (CTC Viterbi over `jonatasgrosman/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-portuguese`, words normalised to the model's letters) and writes `NARR['<storyId>:<index>']` = `[[startMs,endMs],…]`, one pair per `[data-s]` element in the order the page text renders (tagged phrases are one element; untagged words one each). Run it in the background and wait.
   - Re-record a page the build flags (LATE-START, LONG-TAIL, a gap over 1.6 s) or where a word is skipped, repeated or mispronounced (transcribe each with `audio/decode.py`); the voice sometimes speaks the instruction tag aloud — re-record that too.
   - **The title:** record the book title on its own (same voice, model and tag), check it with `audio/decode.py` and its loudness profile (single-word recordings and titles are sometimes said twice: keep only the first saying), trim the silence, and append it to `audio/titles.mp3` (0.4 s silence between titles). Add its `[start,end]` seconds to `TCLIP` under the story id, add the title to the dict in `audio/titlealign.py`, and align its words (cut the title's span, `align.align` on the title words, shift by the span start) into `TWORDS` as `[startMs,endMs]` per title word.
7. **Word sounds (tap a word):** each tappable form (the shown text, lower-cased) needs its own clean recording — a word cut from a sentence is unclear (*olha* was incomprehensible).
   - Record only the forms not yet in `WCLIP` (check every form in the new pages, known keys too: *olhos* had no clip until book 14), in short batches of about 10–15 comma-separated words, same voice and tag. Long lists make the voice loop, repeat words or speak the tag. Add each batch to `audio/wordbatches.json`.
   - `audio/cutwords.py <batch>` aligns each batch and cuts each form into its own clip (fade in/out, mono 48 kbit/s). A form it reports as too short (*nó*) gets its own single recording, trimmed to the first saying.
   - Check every clip: transcribe it with `audio/decode.py` (the recogniser) and scan it for a silence over 0.35 s inside it, which means a second saying or a neighbouring word slipped in. Keep only the one saying of the right word. The recogniser's spelling slips (*azas*, *em costa*) are fine when the sound is right.
   - Append the new clips to `audio/words.mp3` (one file; 0.35 s silence between clips, concatenated with ffmpeg `-c copy`) and add each form's `[start,end]` seconds to `WCLIP`. Never publish the clips as separate files (file limits).
8. **Echoes (since book 16):** when a page reports what someone said, add an `ECHO` entry (`tools/b16/echo16.py`): `{"<storyId>:<index>": [{"id": "eco_<storyId>_<index>_<n>", "ph": <the reported phrase exactly as in the spoken page text>, "o": <the original line>, "pairs": [[[original words], [reported words]], …], "g": [six meanings]}]}`. The reader wraps the phrase in a dotted-underlined `.echo`; tapping it shows the original line above the text with each changed pair in matching colours, plays its clip (record the original line like a word and add it to `WCLIP` under its lower-cased text), and lights the picture's region whose keys are the echo ids (the faded inset). Long-press / Ctrl-click shows the `g` meaning (stored in `GLOSS` under the id). Listen with Slow on shows the original as the phrase is read. A book with three or more echoes gets one extra quiz question («Which sentence reports these words?»). A text-only echo (no inset) is fine when the original never appeared (book 15 page 3: «Eu trago o vinho» → *disse que trazia o vinho*). Books 7 (pages 2, 7) have reported speech without echoes, on purpose (written before the rung).
9. Interface strings live in `UI` (six languages); a new book needs no new strings unless a new feature does. **Page CSS goes in the page's own `<style>` block after `<body>`.** A downloaded copy of the artifact also has a wrapper `<style>` inside `<head>`; publishing throws that one away, so anything put there disappears from the published page (this hid every quiz picture once).
9. Run the checks:
   - scripts parse (`node --check`);
   - every page opens in Playwright, with no 404s or console errors;
   - every word lights the right thing, and every action cue plays the right way (montage the cue screenshots and look);
   - an in-page audit: every page has a `NARR` entry with exactly as many timings as `[data-s]` elements; every `[shown|key]` has a six-language `GLOSS` entry, a `WCLIP` entry for its form, and appears in its page's `PHOTOS` picture;
   - opening the new book from the library is silent, and Listen on page 1 reads the title (title words lit) then the page;
   - Listen with Slow on and off: the highlight keeps pace; with Slow on action words slow down and play full cues, with Slow off only new action words play a short cue; Whole book turns to the next page and opens Words & quiz after the last;
   - tapping each new word plays exactly that word once;
   - Words & quiz for the new book: every new word has a close-up, and quizzes have 8 questions with pictures;
   - thumbnails load; Ctrl/⌘/⌥-click shows each new word's meaning;
   - on page entry the picture and text are both on screen at 1366×650, 1920×1080 and 390×844;
   - after any style change, test a copy with the head `<style>` removed, the way the published page loads;
   - the packed build (`split.py`, Project facts "Testing the build") opens the new book on desktop and a Pixel 7 phone size, with the cover, photos and narration loading.
