## The look, in one paragraph
Book 1 is the standard. Its two defining features: **clean fine linework** and **soft watercolor color**. Every shape has a clean, continuous, dark ink outline of steady medium-fine weight, like a well-inked comic page: never faint, grey, sketchy or broken. That clean line is what makes small details (fingers, coins, polka dots, fruit, railings, tiles) read precisely. Inside the outlines, color is a smooth, nearly flat, transparent watercolor fill with soft shading and white paper left for highlights, with no brush streaks, fabric striations, grain or mottled texture. New pictures have tended to drift toward faint sketchy grey lines, streaky painterly texture, and darker, denser color. Everything below is there to prevent that. (Lesson learned: asking for "thin" lines made Krea draw faint, sketchy ones; ask for "clean, continuous, dark" lines instead.)

## Fixed rules
- **Car makers' badges:** Krea paints real brand badges (VW, Vauxhall) on grilles and steering wheels even with "logos" in the negative prompt. Remove each with a regional edit ("replace the round car-maker badge with a plain smooth grille with no badge or emblem"); one edit per page also handles another small fix (book 12).
- **Model:** `krea/krea-3` (Krea MCP `generate_image`) by default, for every new page and edit. Use another model only when the user asks for it by name for a specific edit (they have used `google/nano-banana-2` for a one-off edit: fields `prompt`, `image_urls`, `aspect_ratio`, `resolution`; no `regional_edit`, seed or negative prompt, so state in the prompt everything that must stay the same). Show the result and check it with Step 4 like any other picture.
- **Review as a set.** For a new book, generate and self-check (Step 4) every page, then show the user all pages together in one contact sheet and redo only the pages they mark. Don't stop for a verdict after each page. For a single page redo, show that page and wait for the verdict.
- **Settings:** `thinking_mode: "fast"` for most pages ("high" rewrites the prompt, pulls the camera back and drifts from the references; it helped for complex physical setups like a tandem launch, books 13–14), `resolution: "2K"` (always send it; a regional edit without it fails with "job needs width/height or resolution"), `aspect_ratio: "16:9"`. A "Bad Gateway" error is transient: send the same call again.
- **Style comes from book 1.** Use the style block below verbatim. Never write vague "same style", and never add style words that aren't in the block (e.g. "thin", "colored pencil", "mottled", "textured", "bright", "cosy", "sunny").
- **Every edit carries the style too.** Regional edits always include the edit style sentence (Step 5), so changed areas don't come back in a different hand.
- **Off-style pictures get regenerated, not restyled.** A restyle anchors on the old picture's surface and only gets partway; a fresh generation with the style block gets much closer to book 1 (see Step 5).
- **Faces come from the character sheet.** Whenever Rita, Ana, João, Marta or the dog is in the scene, the character sheet is a reference and the prompt points to it (see Step 3).
- **Consistent within a book.** Outfits and recurring objects keep the same colour and design on every page of one book (and in any dream or memory picture of that day). Across books only the dog's black collar and leash and Rita's woven straw tote are fixed. Check earlier pages of the same book before approving.
- **Consistent across books.** A place, room or object that appeared in an earlier book looks the same and is seen the same way: the same street view, the same flat, the same balcony view, the same sofa, bookcase and bed sizes (see Step 5, "Same place, same view").
- **Show the action the words describe.** If the page text says someone does something (acorda, abre, compra, pede, bebe, sobe, desce, adormece no ombro), the picture should show it happening, not before or after. Check every action against the page text once more before building (book 11 page 8: Ana was asleep leaning away from João; a regional edit put her head on his shoulder). Words still not shown are flagged by `madeira-book-highlights` in `gen/flagged_actions.json`; when (re)drawing such a page, read that file and paint the flagged actions in.
- **Real-world physics in every scene.** Weather and light behave as they really do, and are written into the scene text:
  - a rainbow is opposite the sun (never next to it), soft and partly see-through, colours blending red outside to violet inside, the sky a little lighter inside the arc, a very faint second bow, fading into clouds; puddles may reflect it. Ask for "natural, realistic"; add "perfect evenly striped rainbow, cartoon rainbow" to the negative prompt;
  - in rain, people and animals look wet (a dog's fur dark, clumped, dripping), ground shines, puddles are irregular and follow dips in the paving, never neat circles or ovals; raindrops sit on visors and kart bodywork;
  - the sea matches the weather: stormy = dark, whitecaps, waves breaking on walls; just after a storm = still choppy with scattered whitecaps; calm = smooth;
  - wind bends trees and palms one way and blows hair the same way;
  - shadows fall away from the sun.
  Check these in Step 4 before the user sees the picture. See also "Make it physically right" below.
- **Krea can't write numbers or words.** Any readable face it paints (a thermometer, clock, scale, speedometer, price tag, sign, menu, timetable) comes out garbled ("810", "590", made-up letters), and it can't put a needle or hands on a value the text names. So:
  - in the scene text, ask only for the object's shape and a plain blank face ("a round thermometer dial with a plain cream face, no numbers"), and keep "No text.";
  - after approval, paint the face in code: inpaint the dial's old marks, then draw a clean scale (ticks, a few round numbers, the unit) and the needle or hands at exactly the value the page text says, squashed to the dial's ellipse so it sits in perspective, in dark sepia ink with a red needle, with the frame, frost and surroundings untouched;
  - one design per kind of object across a book (book 6: both thermometers −10 to 40 °C, 270° sweep, needle on 3 °C at the summit and 25 °C in Funchal);
  - a clock face is fine painted by Krea only when the time is unreadable or doesn't matter; if the text gives a time, draw the hands in code;
  - podiums, karts and race suits: ask for "no numbers", "no writing on it" (book 11);
  - a car's dashboard display: ask for a blank light-grey display, then draw the reading in code (book 12 page 3: "200 000 km" in DejaVu Sans Bold 31 px at 2736 px width, dark sepia, rotated −4.5° to the display's tilt);
  - exception: a short hand-painted sign in capital letters came out correctly at `thinking_mode: "high"` (book 14 page 7, «RITA, ÉS UMA HEROÍNA!», with "The sign reads exactly, in neat hand-painted capital letters: …" and "misspelled text, extra text" in the negative prompt). Zoom in and check every letter.
- **Modern Madeira, the 2010s** (the user's choice): every scene is present-day. Cars and buses are 2010s models (small modern hatchbacks, the city's modern buses), car dashboards have a small touchscreen, people use smartphones (alarms, messages, photos, maps, paying), cafés and shops take cards, and the city shows its modern side where it fits (modern apartment blocks and hotels among the old houses, the marina, the waterfront avenue, the airport, glass-fronted shops) next to what still stands today (old-town cobbles, azulejos, the market, basket sledges, the cable car). Write the era into the scene text ("a modern small hatchback from the 2010s"; "her smartphone"; "modern white apartment buildings among the old houses") and add "retro car, vintage car, 1970s, cassette radio" to the negative prompt when a vehicle appears.
- **Two interior pages in a row** (book 12 pages 4–5): generate the second with the approved first as the third reference ("The same car interior, seats, people and camera position as the third image") and the same seed, and write every outfit into the prompt from the start. A clothing edit on a person seen from behind (overalls over the back) merged him into the seat; redraw rather than edit.
- **Things on the ground:** check that vehicles and people stand on flat ground; Krea put a car's rear wheel on garden steps (book 12 page 6). A regional edit replacing the steps with "a flat, level cobbled driveway" fixed it.
- **People in a moving vehicle** follow `madeira-vehicle-interior` as well (camera choice, seats, belts, left-hand drive, real geography, the same car on every page of a book). A parked van or car facing the camera on a Portuguese street has its driver's door on the street side (left-hand drive).
- **Believable scene geometry.** Vehicles sit on the street with wheels on the ground and in perspective with the buildings; people are at a realistic scale for their distance from the camera; views through windows match the outside world. If an outside view looks wrong, try an inside view looking forward instead.
- **References:** 3 at most, in this order:
  1. book 1 page 7 (`b1p7.jpg`) for style,
  2. the character sheet (`character-sheet-v4.jpg`) when any recurring character appears,
  3. one scene reference: book 1 page 5 (daytime outdoors), page 3 (indoors) or page 9 (night), or the earlier book's page when the scene returns to the same place, or a photo of the real place (Step 5, "Real places"), or a design sheet / approved page showing a recurring boat, vehicle or person not on the character sheet (Sofia, Duarte). For a car interior use no third reference (see `madeira-vehicle-interior`).
  Too many references average out the style and the faces.

## Step 1 — get the page
Read the artifact. The page texts live in `pages1`, `pages2`, …; words in `[shown|key]` brackets are the tappable vocabulary. Every tappable word's thing must be clearly visible in the picture (e.g. the sun for "sol", a visible shadow for "sombra", a tired pose for "cansadas"), every action word visibly happening, and the story object large in the foreground. Read `gen/flagged_actions.json` for this page.

**Paint for clean highlights.** Each tappable thing becomes an exact cut-out when tapped (`madeira-book-highlights`), so compose the scene so every one can be cut cleanly:
- each tappable thing fully visible and in one piece — not hidden behind a person, a hand or another object (no hand covering the coins, no person standing across the window, no cat merging into the wall);
- set against a contrasting background, with a little space around it;
- the sun or moon a clear separate disc in open sky, away from cliffs, rooftops and window frames;
- anything a word names shown big enough to tap (at least ~5% of the picture width): a street seen from a bus, a road, a cup, coins;
- people not overlapping each other more than the scene needs, and the dog with clear space around it;
- for words about the whole place (praia, mar, floresta, cidade, casa, kartódromo), show the place broadly with the characters inside it, not covering it;
- a moving person or animal (an action cue word) with clear ground around it, so a clean plate can be made.
Put these needs into the scene text in plain words (e.g. "the coins clearly visible in her open palm", "the sun a separate disc high in the clear sky").

## Step 2 — references into Krea
The uploads go from the workspace to krea.ai, so the workspace needs krea.ai on its network allowlist. If a request fails with a proxy 403, tell the user and stop; don't retry with other tools.
1. `mcp__Krea__list_files` (scope user): reuse files already uploaded (b1p1.jpg … b1p9.jpg); the v4 character sheet's lasting URL is in `tools/charsheet/README.md`.
2. Missing ones: download from the artifact (`Artifact read` with `paths`), then `mcp__Krea__write_files_upload` → POST raw bytes: `curl -sS -X POST --data-binary @file.jpg -H 'Content-Type: image/jpeg' "<uploadUrl>"` (never `-F`).
3. Right before generating, `mcp__Krea__read_files` on the file URIs for download URLs. They expire after 5 minutes, so fetch them in the same turn as the generate call.
4. Easier, lasting reference URLs: `mcp__Krea__get_upload_url` (valid 3 hours, reusable for many files), then `curl -sS -X POST "$URL" -F "file=@b1p7.jpg"` returns an `https://app-uploads.krea.ai/...` URL that does not expire; note them in a `refs.txt` and reuse them all session (book 1 page 7 is `https://app-uploads.krea.ai/c0d188ce-8c97-43b3-aef4-d59c0893f90f/1792558460001-18e4c251-5d8a-431e-9307-c14a4d665aea-b1p7.jpeg`). The URL carries the full original file name (`…-<uuid>-<original-name>.jpeg`); a shortened one 404s and the generation fails at once, so copy it exactly.
5. A previous Krea result can be used directly as a reference or edit source by its `https://gen.krea.ai/images/...png` URL. Note each approved page's Krea URL; animation variants and clean plates are edits of it.

## Step 3 — the prompt
Prompt = style block + face line + characters present + scene. Always send the negative prompt.

**Style block (verbatim):**
```
Pen-and-ink illustration with soft watercolor, in exactly the style of the first reference image. Inking: every shape, foreground and background, gets a clean, continuous, dark sepia-black ink outline of steady medium-fine weight, like the outlines around the woman, her hands, the coins and the bananas in the first image; never faint, grey, sketchy or broken. Small details such as fingers, polka dots, fruit, coins, railings, tiles, cobbles and fur edges are drawn precisely with that same clean line. Light parallel hatching only in hair strands, wood grain and deep shadows. Coloring: smooth, nearly flat, transparent watercolor fills inside the outlines, soft shading, white paper left for highlights; no brush streaks, no fabric striations, no grain or mottled texture on clothes, ground, sand or sky. Colors soft and slightly muted. Palette: cobalt blue, bottle green, terracotta, honey-brown wood, cream white, soft teal sea, pale blue sky with white clouds. Soft natural daylight, gentle shadows. Realistic adult proportions and faces, small natural features. Background in the same clean line with paler washes. 16:9, eye-level view, the story object large and clear in the foreground.
```
For night: replace "Soft natural daylight, gentle shadows." with "Night: soft, transparent deep-navy washes, still light enough that the clean outlines read clearly, lit by one warm lamp and the moon."

**Face line (verbatim, whenever a recurring character appears):**
```
The characters' faces, hair and the dog must match the character sheet (the second image) exactly: row 1 is Rita; in row 2 the first two tiles are Ana and the last two are the dog; row 3 is João; row 4 is Marta; row 5 is Tiago; in row 6 the first two tiles are Sofia and the last two are Duarte; in row 7 the first two tiles are Inês and the last two are Bruno. Same face shape, features, age and hair as in those tiles; ignore the clothes, caps, helmets and visors in the tiles.
```
**Name only the rows of the people present** ("row 1 is Rita; row 5 is Tiago"); a full list invites extra people. **When the dog is not in the scene**, also add "(ignore the dog tiles: there is no dog in this picture)", and put "dog, corgi, animals" in the negative prompt. Otherwise Krea adds Ana's corgi (book 11: on three of eight pages). A regional edit removes a stray dog reliably.

**Outfits fit the story** (the user's rule): faces, hair and the dog never change; clothes and their colours are free and are chosen for each book to suit the place, season, weather, activity and mood, the way real people dress (beach, hike, rain, dinner out, office, flat viewing, sport). Guidelines:
- choose both women's outfits together so their colours complement each other and stand out from the background (e.g. terracotta against teal, mustard against navy, sage against rust); avoid both in the same colour, and avoid colours that disappear into the scene (green on green cliffs, black on black sand);
- stay within the soft, slightly muted palette of the style block; patterns are fine when simple (polka dots, stripes, small checks);
- write each outfit into the character line, keep it identical on every page of the book, and add it to "Outfits so far" once the book's pictures are approved; add any clothing that must not appear to the negative prompt (e.g. "polka dot dress" when Rita isn't wearing one);

## Make it physically right (the user's corrections, books 13–14)
Before generating, write down for each page where the camera stands, which way everyone faces and moves, and how the main objects work; put that in the prompt in plain words ("seen exactly side-on from the left; Rita in front on the right, Duarte directly behind her"). Then check the result against it. What went wrong before:
- **One fixed design for a recurring vehicle or boat.** Make a design sheet first (the boat side-on, on a plain background) and pass it as the third reference on every page, saying "the boat is exactly the boat in the third image". Keep the user's choice (book 13: a modern white sloop, not a classic wooden boat). Choose the reference that shows the right state: a sail-cover reference produced sails both furled and hoisted — use a page where the boat is sailing as the reference for sailing pages.
- **People must be possible.** No legs hidden in a deck or cut off by a rail: say "their whole bodies visible from head to feet, feet planted on the deck". Someone "standing in the cockpit" stands in it, not floating. Seated passengers sit inside the guardrails, feet on the cockpit floor, never dangling over the side.
- **Things face and move the right way.** A paraglider taking off flies away from the slope over the sea (seen from behind); dolphins swim alongside a boat in its direction, parallel to the hull, not toward it; a boat moored stern-to shows its stern to the pontoon on every page.
- **Animals at a natural size and distance.** Two or three animals, in varied poses (one leaping, one breathing at the surface, one under the water), drawn partly submerged with wakes — not whole bodies sitting on top of the water, not oversized, not identical. Birds near people stay small and well away (a buzzard about thirty metres off reads as natural; one beside a face reads as frightening).
- **Tandem paragliding (the user's rules):** the instructor wears the big pilot harness and holds the brakes; the passenger wears a light seat harness and is clipped directly IN FRONT of him by spreader bars at hip height. They run together one behind the other on launch and on landing. ALL the wing's lines gather into two risers that clip to the pilot's harness at his shoulders — none go to the passenger. - **Background people are real people:** each with a different gender, age, build, skin and clothes, never identical copies.
- **Heeled boats, hard turns:** ask for a strong lean in degrees ("about 20 degrees, the mast slanting clearly"); a regenerated whole picture may swap the women's outfits — check and swap back with a regional edit.

## Step 5 — edits and fixes
**Edit style sentence (verbatim, at the end of every edit prompt, before "Keep everything else the same."):**
```
Draw everything you change with the same clean, continuous dark ink outlines and the same smooth, nearly flat, transparent watercolor fills as the rest of the image, with no brush streaks or texture.
```
- **Small or local fixes** (a face, hair, a pose, a prop's size or colour, clothing colours, the ground, an extra or missing object, a stray mark): use `regional_edit: true` with `thinking_mode: "fast"` and `resolution: "2K"`, the image to fix as the first `image_urls` entry, the instruction in plain words, the edit style sentence, then "Keep everything else the same." Regional edits keep the rest pixel-exact, so they can be chained. Mark the area with `regions` (boxes in 0–1 coordinates on `image_idx` 0, each with a `label` and a `note` saying what goes there) so the edit stays inside it; use `thinking_mode: "high"` for anything harder than a recolour (moving lines to the right harness, lifting feet off the ground, redrawing animals). `timeoutSeconds` is at most 300.
- **Face fix:** add the character sheet as the second image and say e.g. "Make the face and hair of the woman with straight dark hair and bangs match the top row of the second image."
- **When an edit asks for something partial** (e.g. "no sand on the concrete except in the corners by the kiosk"), write that exception into the instruction and edit from the last good version rather than stacking another edit on a bad one.
- **Background landscape changes** (open a V between hills to show the sea, remove a hill, make a slope volcanic): one regional edit can do it when it says exactly where ("just above the fence behind the young man in the grey hoodie") and keeps the people, track, grass and sky. A vague first try roughened the whole picture and added nothing. When the user asks for the same idea as a new image, a fresh generation describing the new landscape in plain words gave the best page (book 11 page 1).
- **An older or off-style picture** (faint or sketchy lines, streaky texture, dark color): regenerate it from scratch with the full Step 3 prompt, describing the same scene in words (and any composition details the user approved, like the concrete pad or cone size). This beat restyling clearly on book 2 page 6. A restyle (no `regional_edit`, the picture first and book 1 page 7 second, "Redraw the first image with the inking and coloring of the second image … keep the composition, people, poses, faces and objects exactly as they are") keeps the exact composition but only moves the style partway; offer it only when the user wants that exact composition kept.
- **What edits can't do** (learned on book 9 page 1, many tries): a regional edit will not rotate an object, turn a statue around, move a person to another seat or change a camera angle; it redraws in place. Write those into a fresh generation instead. When a picture keeps failing, don't pile corrections into the prompt: start again with a short, clean prompt (style + faces + a plain description of the scene), add each fix as one plain sentence, and use the last good picture as the third reference only if its layout is right. Ask for one change per edit. (A pose change in place does work: leaning a sleeping person's head onto a neighbour's shoulder, book 11 page 8.)

Its edits also confuse who is who (it duplicated João and gave Marta Ana's hair); check the people count and faces every time.
